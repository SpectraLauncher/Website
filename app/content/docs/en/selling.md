A project can be sold rather than given away. Payments run through Stripe, and
what a buyer pays lands on Spectra's account before it is passed on to you.

## Setting up

Connect a payout account under **Selling**. Identity checks, tax details and
bank details live on Stripe's side — this service never sees an account number
or a document.

You can put a project on sale before that verification finishes. Money from
those sales waits as **pending** and moves to you once the account is ready, so
nothing is lost by starting early.

The account has to be in a country money can reach from here: the EEA, the UK,
Switzerland, the United States or Canada. Anywhere else is refused when you
connect rather than after verification, because a transfer would be impossible.

## Setting a price

Under a project's settings, open **Pricing** and switch it to paid. Prices are
in euro; the minimum is €3.

The tab shows what you take home before you save, which matters more than the
rate does:

| Price | Fee | You get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| €3.00 | €0.50 | €2.50 |
| €5.00 | €0.50 | €4.50 |
| €10.00 | €0.80 | €9.20 |
| €25.00 | €2.00 | €23.00 |

## The fee

We take 8% of a sale and never less than €0.50. Partners and verified
organizations pay 6%.

That single fee covers payment processing too — Stripe does not bill you
separately. Because of the floor, cheap items pay a higher effective percentage:
at €3 the fee is €0.50, which is about 17%.

In a cart holding several creators' work the fee is worked out per item, so a
cheap project never subsidises an expensive one.

## Organizations

An organization has no account of its own and holds nothing. Sales of its
projects are divided between members and paid straight to their own accounts.

An owner or admin sets the shares under **Split** in the organization's
settings. They must add up to 100%. Without a split, the owners take everything.

A member with no payout account still earns their share; it waits as pending
until they connect one.

## What buyers get

Access opens the moment payment succeeds, whatever is still waiting to move
between accounts. A purchase covers every version of that project, including
ones released later, and it does not expire.

If you stop publishing a project, the people who bought it keep what they
bought.
