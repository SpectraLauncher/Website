Money from a sale reaches you in two steps, and they are worth telling apart.

## Pending, then transferred

A sale first appears as **pending** on your revenue page. It stays there for a
holding period counted from the payment, which gives a chargeback or a licence
complaint time to surface while the money is still on Spectra's account.

Once that passes, the amount is transferred to your Stripe account and shows as
**transferred**. From that point Stripe holds it, not us.

The ledger only ever has those two states. We do not run a wallet and we never
hold your money as a balance of our own.

## Getting it to your bank

Payouts are manual. Stripe will not move anything to your bank on a schedule —
you start it yourself with **Pay out** on the revenue page.

Two limits apply:

- A minimum payout, because below it the transfer costs more than it moves.
- Stripe requires funds sitting on a connected account to be paid out within 90
  days (longer in the United States). Do not leave them there indefinitely.

The amount shown as available comes from Stripe, not from our ledger. Refunds,
reserves and Stripe's own settlement timing all move that number in ways our
rows never see.

## When a buyer disputes a payment

A chargeback is started by the buyer's bank, and no terms can prevent one.

The amount appears in your ledger as a negative entry and reduces what we owe
you. Later sales work it off before anything is transferred, so a dispute does
not produce a bill — it produces a slower next payout. The buyer loses download
access while the dispute is open, and gets it back if it is resolved in our
favour.

## If something looks wrong

Every entry in the ledger says what it was for, which sale it came from, and
your share where a split applied. Nothing is ever edited or deleted; a
correction is another entry. If a number does not add up, that history is what
we will both be reading.
